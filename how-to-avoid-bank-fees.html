How to Avoid Bank Fees and Keep More of Your Money

✍️ MoneyGuide Editorial • 📅 August 3, 2026 • ⏱️ 8 min read

Bank fees may seem small, but they can add up quickly over time. Learning how banks charge fees—and how to avoid them—can help you save hundreds of dollars every year without changing your financial habits dramatically.

Why Banks Charge Fees

Banks earn money in many ways, including service charges. Common fees include monthly maintenance fees, ATM fees, overdraft charges, foreign transaction fees, and wire transfer fees. Understanding these charges allows you to choose accounts that fit your needs.

Avoid Monthly Maintenance Fees

Many banks waive monthly account fees if you maintain a minimum balance, receive direct deposits, or meet other account requirements. Before opening an account, compare fee policies carefully.

💡 MoneyGuide Tip

Choose banks that offer no-fee checking accounts or waive fees through simple requirements like direct deposit.

Reduce ATM Charges

Using out-of-network ATMs often results in additional charges from both your bank and the ATM operator. Whenever possible, use your bank's ATM network or request cash back during purchases.

Prevent Overdraft Fees

Overdraft fees occur when you spend more money than is available in your checking account. Monitoring your account balance regularly and enabling low-balance alerts can help prevent these expensive charges.

Important: Overdraft fees can sometimes exceed $30 per transaction. Keeping track of your available balance is one of the easiest ways to avoid unnecessary costs.

Common Bank Fees

Fee Type What Causes It How to Avoid It
Monthly Maintenance Fee Not meeting account requirements Maintain minimum balance or use direct deposit
ATM Fee Using an out-of-network ATM Use your bank's ATM network
Overdraft Fee Spending more than your balance Monitor balance and enable alerts
Foreign Transaction Fee Purchases made in another currency Use cards with no foreign transaction fees
Wire Transfer Fee Sending domestic or international wires Compare lower-cost transfer alternatives

More Ways to Save Money

Many online banks offer accounts with fewer fees than traditional banks. Comparing account features before opening a new account can save you money over the long term.

It's also a good idea to review your monthly bank statements. Unexpected fees may appear, and identifying them early allows you to contact your bank or adjust your banking habits before additional charges occur.

💡 Quick Tip

Set up mobile banking notifications so you'll receive alerts whenever money enters or leaves your account.

Common Banking Mistakes

  • Ignoring monthly bank statements.
  • Using out-of-network ATMs regularly.
  • Allowing your account balance to become negative.
  • Not comparing banks before opening an account.
  • Paying for services you rarely use.
"Keeping more of your money isn't just about earning more—it's also about paying fewer unnecessary fees."

Conclusion

Avoiding bank fees doesn't require complicated financial knowledge. By choosing the right bank account, monitoring your balance, using in-network ATMs, and understanding your bank's policies, you can reduce unnecessary expenses and keep more of your hard-earned money throughout the year.

Key Takeaways

  • Understand your bank's fee schedule.
  • Use ATMs within your bank's network.
  • Prevent overdrafts by monitoring your balance.
  • Review monthly statements regularly.
  • Compare banks to find lower-fee accounts.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can banks waive certain fees?

Yes. Many banks will waive maintenance fees if you meet account requirements or request a fee reversal after a one-time mistake.

Are online banks usually cheaper?

Many online banks offer lower fees because they have lower operating costs than traditional branch-based banks.
