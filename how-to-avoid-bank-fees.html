How to Avoid Bank Fees and Keep More of Your Money

✍️ MoneyGuide Editorial • 📅 August 2026 • ⏱️ 12 min read

Bank fees can be easy to overlook because many individual charges appear relatively small. A monthly maintenance fee, an occasional ATM charge, or an overdraft fee might not seem significant on its own. However, repeated charges can gradually reduce the amount of money available for saving, investing, debt repayment, and everyday expenses. The good news is that many common banking fees can be avoided with a little planning. Understanding your account requirements, monitoring your balance, choosing the right ATM, reviewing your statements, and comparing different bank accounts can make a meaningful difference over time.

Why Banks Charge Fees

Banks charge fees for a variety of services and account features. These charges can help financial institutions cover operating costs, provide specialized services, or manage certain types of account activity. Common charges may include monthly maintenance fees, out-of-network ATM fees, overdraft fees, wire transfer fees, foreign transaction fees, returned payment fees, and fees for certain specialized services. Not every bank charges the same fees, and some accounts have conditions that allow customers to avoid certain charges. This means that understanding the fee schedule for your particular account is an important part of managing your banking costs.

Before opening a checking or savings account, take time to examine the account's fee structure. A bank account that looks attractive because of a particular feature may not be the best option if you regularly incur fees for services you use. Likewise, an account with a small monthly fee may still be reasonable if the fee is easily waived by meeting requirements that already fit your normal financial habits.

💡 MoneyGuide Tip

Don't judge a bank account only by its advertised features. Check the complete fee schedule and understand which charges apply to the way you actually use your account.

Avoid Monthly Maintenance Fees

Monthly maintenance fees are recurring charges that some financial institutions apply to checking or savings accounts. Depending on the account, the fee may be waived if you meet specific requirements. For example, an account may waive the fee when you receive qualifying direct deposits, maintain a certain balance, make a required number of transactions, or meet another condition specified by the bank.

The easiest way to avoid this type of fee is to understand exactly what your account requires. If your account has a minimum balance requirement, monitor your balance carefully so that an unexpected withdrawal does not cause you to fall below the required amount. If the account requires direct deposit, make sure your income is deposited according to the bank's eligibility requirements.

Another option is to compare your current account with other checking accounts. If you consistently pay a monthly fee and do not receive enough value from the account to justify it, another account may better match your needs.

Choose a Bank Account That Matches Your Habits

One of the most effective ways to reduce banking costs is to choose an account based on how you actually manage money. Someone who regularly uses ATMs may prioritize a bank with a large ATM network. Someone who rarely visits branches may prefer an account that emphasizes online banking and low account fees. A person who frequently receives direct deposits may prefer an account where that activity automatically eliminates a monthly charge.

The important point is that there is no universally perfect bank account. The best option depends on your income, spending patterns, preferred banking services, ATM usage, balance, and financial priorities.

Reduce ATM Charges

ATM fees are another common banking expense. When you use an ATM outside your bank's network, you may be charged by the ATM operator and potentially by your own financial institution. These charges can become particularly frustrating when you withdraw small amounts of cash. Paying a fee to access a relatively small amount of money can make the effective cost of the transaction surprisingly high.

Whenever possible, use ATMs that belong to your bank or its participating network. Many banking apps include ATM locators that can help you find nearby machines that are less likely to result in additional fees.

Some accounts may also reimburse certain ATM fees, although the terms vary. If ATM access is important to you, consider this feature when comparing accounts. Another simple strategy is to plan cash withdrawals rather than repeatedly stopping at different ATMs. Combining errands and withdrawing an appropriate amount can reduce the number of ATM transactions you make.

💡 Quick Tip

Before using an unfamiliar ATM, check the screen for any surcharge information. If the fee seems unnecessary, cancel the transaction and look for another ATM.

Prevent Overdraft Fees

Overdraft fees can happen when transactions cause your checking account balance to fall below the amount available to cover them. Depending on the account and transaction, this can result in additional charges. The best way to prevent overdraft costs is to actively monitor your available balance instead of relying only on the balance shown in your account.

Remember that pending transactions, automatic payments, checks, debit card purchases, and other scheduled transactions can affect how much money is actually available. Creating a small buffer in your checking account can also provide additional protection against unexpected timing differences between deposits and withdrawals.

Important: Don't assume that having money in your account always means the same amount is available for immediate spending. Pending transactions and scheduled payments can affect your available funds.

Use Banking Alerts to Catch Problems Early

Banking alerts are a simple tool that can help you stay aware of account activity. Depending on your bank, you may be able to receive notifications when your balance falls below a certain amount, when a transaction occurs, or when money is deposited.

These notifications can help you identify unusual activity quickly and make it easier to recognize when your balance is getting close to a level that could cause problems. Instead of checking your account manually several times a day, you can use alerts to highlight situations that require attention.

Understand Foreign Transaction Fees

Foreign transaction fees can become important when you travel internationally or make purchases from merchants that process transactions in another currency. These charges can vary depending on the financial product you use. Before traveling or making international purchases, review the terms associated with your checking account, debit card, and credit card.

If international spending is a regular part of your financial life, comparing products based on foreign transaction costs may help reduce unnecessary expenses. However, don't choose a financial product based on one fee alone. Consider the complete set of fees, features, security protections, exchange-rate policies, and other terms.

Be Careful With Wire Transfer Fees

Wire transfers can be useful when you need to move money quickly or send funds to another financial institution. However, they may carry fees depending on the bank, transfer type, destination, and other circumstances. Before sending a wire, check the total cost and confirm that the recipient information is correct. A mistake can be difficult or costly to reverse.

For routine transfers that do not require a wire, compare whether another transfer method offered by your financial institution is more appropriate and less expensive.

Review Your Bank Statements Every Month

One of the easiest ways to identify unnecessary fees is to review your bank statement regularly. A statement can show recurring charges that you may have forgotten about, unexpected account fees, ATM charges, or other costs that are easy to overlook when you focus only on your account balance.

Take a few minutes each month to scan your statement. Look for charges that you do not recognize and fees that could potentially be avoided. If you notice an unfamiliar transaction, contact your financial institution using its official customer service channels as soon as practical.

💡 MoneyGuide Tip

Don't just check your balance. Review the actual transactions and fees appearing on your statement so you know exactly where your money is going.

Consider Online Banking Options

Online banking can provide convenient access to checking and savings accounts without requiring frequent branch visits. Some online-focused institutions may offer accounts with fewer traditional service fees. However, lower fees should not automatically be interpreted as better. Before opening an account, consider ATM access, customer support, deposit options, transfer policies, security features, interest rates, and any requirements attached to fee waivers.

The right banking setup should make it easy for you to access your money, monitor transactions, and manage everyday financial tasks without repeatedly paying for services you need.

Ask Whether a Fee Can Be Waived

If you notice a fee on your account, don't automatically assume that there is nothing you can do about it. Some banks may have policies that allow certain fees to be waived or reversed under particular circumstances. Whether a fee can be removed depends entirely on the institution, account terms, fee type, and situation.

When contacting your bank, clearly explain what happened and ask whether the charge can be reviewed. Even if a fee cannot be reversed, the conversation may help you understand how to prevent the same charge in the future.

Watch for Recurring Banking Services

Some banking customers pay for additional services they rarely use. These could include optional account features, specialized statement services, or other products attached to an account. Review your account periodically and ask whether every paid feature still provides value. If you no longer need a service, determine whether it can be removed without affecting the core functionality of your account.

Common Banking Mistakes That Lead to Fees

Bank fees are not always caused by complicated financial decisions. In many cases, they come from simple habits that can be corrected once you know what to look for.

  • Ignoring monthly bank statements.
  • Using out-of-network ATMs regularly.
  • Allowing your checking balance to become too low.
  • Forgetting about automatic payments.
  • Not comparing bank accounts before opening one.
  • Paying for services you rarely use.
  • Failing to check account requirements for fee waivers.
  • Ignoring notifications about low balances or transactions.
  • Using expensive transfer methods when lower-cost options may be available.
  • Assuming every bank has the same fee structure.

Build a Simple Fee-Reduction Routine

You don't need to spend hours managing your bank accounts. A simple monthly routine can help you catch unnecessary charges before they become a recurring problem. Once a month, review your checking and savings balances, scan recent transactions, check for unexpected fees, and confirm that upcoming automatic payments are covered.

Every few months, review whether your account still fits your needs. Your financial situation can change over time. A bank account that worked well several years ago may no longer be the most cost-effective option.

Connect Banking With Your Monthly Budget

Banking fees are easier to prevent when you understand your overall monthly cash flow. A simple budget can show when your income arrives, when bills are withdrawn, how much money is available for everyday spending, and how much should remain in your account as a buffer.

When you combine budgeting with account alerts, automatic transfers, and regular statement reviews, it becomes easier to recognize potential problems before they result in fees.

A Simple Bank Fee Checklist

Use the following checklist whenever you review your banking setup:

  • Check whether your checking account has a monthly maintenance fee.
  • Understand exactly how the fee can be waived.
  • Confirm which ATMs are inside your bank's network.
  • Enable low-balance and transaction alerts.
  • Review pending transactions before making large purchases.
  • Check your monthly statement for unexpected fees.
  • Review international transaction costs before traveling.
  • Compare transfer costs before sending money.
  • Ask your bank whether an unexpected fee can be reviewed.
  • Compare your current account with alternatives periodically.

Why Small Fees Matter Over the Long Term

A single small fee may not have a major effect on your financial situation. The bigger concern is repeated fees. Money that leaves your account unnecessarily cannot be used for other financial goals. It could otherwise remain available for an emergency fund, debt repayment, savings, investing, or everyday expenses.

For example, someone who repeatedly pays avoidable charges throughout the year may lose more money than they realize because the fees become part of their normal financial routine. Reducing unnecessary fees is therefore less about obsessing over every small transaction and more about eliminating recurring costs that provide little or no value.

Banking Habits Worth Changing

Improving your banking habits does not necessarily mean changing banks immediately. Sometimes a few small adjustments can make your existing account significantly easier and cheaper to manage.

  • Check your balance before making large purchases.
  • Keep track of automatic bills and subscriptions.
  • Use account alerts to monitor important activity.
  • Know your bank's ATM network.
  • Review your fee schedule at least periodically.
  • Keep emergency savings separate from everyday spending when appropriate.
  • Don't ignore repeated fees simply because each charge seems small.
"Keeping more of your money isn't just about earning more—it's also about paying fewer unnecessary fees."

Conclusion

Avoiding bank fees does not require complicated financial strategies. The process starts with understanding how your account works and identifying the charges that apply to your normal banking habits. Using in-network ATMs, maintaining an appropriate account balance, enabling alerts, reviewing statements, understanding fee-waiver requirements, and comparing banking products can all help reduce unnecessary costs.

The most important step is to become familiar with the terms of your own account. Every financial institution can have different rules, so never assume that a fee policy at one bank is identical to another.

Over time, reducing recurring fees can free up money that can be redirected toward more useful goals such as building an emergency fund, paying down debt, saving for a major purchase, or investing for the future.

Key Takeaways

  • Understand your bank's complete fee schedule.
  • Choose accounts that match your everyday banking habits.
  • Use ATMs within your bank's network whenever possible.
  • Monitor your available balance to help prevent overdrafts.
  • Enable low-balance and transaction alerts.
  • Review monthly statements for unexpected charges.
  • Compare transfer and international transaction costs.
  • Ask your bank whether eligible fees can be reviewed or waived.
  • Periodically compare your current account with other options.
  • Redirect money saved from unnecessary fees toward important financial goals.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can banks waive certain fees?

Some banks may waive or reverse certain fees depending on their policies and the circumstances. If you notice an unexpected charge, you can contact your bank and ask whether the fee can be reviewed. Even when a fee cannot be reversed, the bank may explain how to prevent similar charges in the future.

Are online banks usually cheaper?

Some online-focused banks offer accounts with fewer traditional banking fees, but costs vary between institutions. When comparing accounts, consider the complete fee structure along with ATM access, customer support, deposit options, transfer policies, and other features.

How can I avoid ATM fees?

Use ATMs within your bank's network whenever possible. You can also check whether your bank participates in a larger ATM network or offers any applicable fee reimbursement. Checking the ATM surcharge before completing a withdrawal can also help you avoid unexpected costs.

How can I avoid overdraft fees?

Monitor your available balance, keep track of automatic payments, and enable low-balance notifications if your bank offers them. Maintaining a reasonable cash buffer can also help protect against unexpected timing differences between deposits and withdrawals.

Should I change banks if I keep paying fees?

It may be worth comparing other accounts if you repeatedly pay fees that are difficult to avoid. Before switching, review the complete fee schedule, ATM access, account requirements, customer service, transfer options, and other features to determine whether another account is genuinely a better fit.

How often should I review my bank account?

You should monitor your account activity regularly and review your monthly statement carefully. A more detailed review every few months can also help you determine whether your current account still matches your financial needs.
